What it is

TimeLocked is an iPhone app for putting something out of your own reach for a while. You lock it, and when you want it back you have to ask and then wait. It's for the version of you who wants it back at 2am.

A Pause tab does the same for a purchase link or a message you'd regret: it waits as long as you chose, then comes back and asks whether you still want it.

The app is in 16 languages, English, Spanish, French, German, Russian, Chinese and Japanese among them.

How it works

  • Name it, put the thing in, pick the wait. Waits run from an hour to 30 days.
  • When you want it back, ask. The wait starts then, and the entry opens when it's over. Nothing opens immediately — that's the point.
  • Lock it again and the next time waits too.
  • The wait is timed by Apple's iCloud servers, not by your phone's clock.
  • Make a wait longer whenever you like and it applies at once. Make it shorter and the new length only takes effect once the old one has passed.

What people lock away

  • A social media password, right after changing it
  • Recovery codes and backup codes
  • A card PIN or a card number
  • The login for a betting site, a shop or a game
  • Photos you keep going back to

What it protects against, and what it doesn't

TimeLocked protects you from you. It is not a safe against someone else: anyone who can unlock your iPhone can open what's past its wait, unless you add a master password.

It is a delay, not a backup. The developer has no server of its own and can't read what you lock, so can't open it for you either. Keep your own offline copy of anything you can't afford to lose.

Where your things are kept

  • What's inside an entry is encrypted with AES-256-GCM the moment you save.
  • The name you give an entry is not encrypted, so don't put the password itself in the name.
  • Entries and their waits live on your phone and in your private iCloud.
  • Photos and voice notes aren't synced to iCloud: deleting the app deletes them, unless you restore from a device backup.
  • For an entry that's in iCloud, deleting the app doesn't end the wait — the key stays in your Keychain and the clock is Apple's.
  • The app opens with Face ID or your iPhone passcode, and you can add a six-digit code of its own. A master password takes the key out of iCloud and puts it behind a password only you know.

Premium

Premium adds two things: closing the apps you pick while something waits, or every night on your schedule, and keeping more than three things in the Vault. Every wait length, and opening what you locked, stays outside Premium.
